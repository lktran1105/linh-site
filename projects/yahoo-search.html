The problem

Data analysis showed a repeated pattern: users added the word "map" to their searches. Someone would search "Los Angeles," then search again for "Los Angeles map." This happened in about 20% of all location-related searches, which pointed to a clear gap between what Yahoo Search showed and what users wanted: a quick, easy way to see a city map.

With a map clearly needed, the next question was which use case to build for. I chose travel for three reasons:

  • Travel is one of the biggest search categories.
  • It has strong follow-up intent. After searching a location, people want things to do, restaurants, and hotels.
  • It's an opportunity to extend the search session.

How do we create a travel experience on Yahoo Map?

Goal: user retention and user experience. Success: Yahoo becomes known as a good search engine for travel and maps.

What I did

I spotted the opportunity, pitched the concept to leadership, and got approval to move forward. From there I set the vision and scope, aligned goals across cross-functional teams, and led the product from 0 to 1, covering ideation, user research, design, development, and iteration. I was the central point of coordination, keeping execution on schedule and stakeholders aligned.

The solution was an interactive map that shows the city and also surfaces attractions, restaurants, and hotels.

Phase 1: Teamwork. Aligned on goals and an action plan with 5 cross-functional teams.

  • Defined the project scope. Goal: show activities, restaurants, and hotels on a city-based map. Timeline: 1 month.
  • Presented the problem statement and goals for feedback.
  • Set up weekly check-ins.

Phase 2: MVP. Built MVP mockups to run 6 user interviews.

  • Partnered with a designer to create two concept designs.
  • Worked with UX researchers to interview users on travel habits and pain points.
  • Collected and analyzed feedback on high-fidelity prototypes.

Phase 3: Iteration. Got leadership approval to refine the project for launch readiness.

  • Presented progress to leadership and 200+ Search team members.
  • Scaled city coverage from 1 to most major US cities.
  • Launched a "delightful feature" to enhance the user experience.
  • Expanded the number of points of interest shown on the map.
  • Introduced "Events" to help users discover local happenings.

How I measured it

I ran a phased rollout to 5%, 10%, 25%, 50%, 70%, and then 100% of users. Each stage ran about 2 weeks to reach statistically significant results. I tracked three metrics:

  • Click-through rate: how often users click on the map module, which measures map usage.
  • Search session (long dwell time): how long users stay on the results page, which shows overall engagement.
  • Scroll-past rate: whether users interact with the map or scroll past it, which shows relevance.

Outcome

  • 40% of users interact with the map module, so it helps them explore.
  • 60% increase in search session length. Users stay on the page longer now that the map is there.
  • Under 20% of users scroll past the module. Most engage with it.
  • 10% increase in interaction across the whole results page. The map keeps users engaged with everything else too.

I presented the project to Search leadership, and it won 1st prize at Yahoo Search Megathon 2023, along with funding for further development.
